AT A GLANCE

Steve Jobs (1955–2011) was an American entrepreneur who helped shape personal technology and computer animation. He co-founded Apple with Steve Wozniak, founded NeXT after leaving Apple, and became a major investor and leader at Pixar. His career included bold product ideas, difficult changes, and close work with talented teams.

A CLOSER LOOK

Ideas made
real

Jobs and Steve Wozniak started Apple in 1976. Their first product, the Apple I, was sold as a circuit board; the Apple II followed as a ready-to-use personal computer with color graphics. The company grew by making computers that people could use at home and work.

Jobs became known for bringing technology, design, and a clear product message together. At Apple, he helped guide the Macintosh, introduced in 1984 with a graphical interface and mouse. His contribution was often to shape the product direction and explain why a product mattered, while engineering and design teams built it.

After leaving Apple in 1985, he founded NeXT, which made computers and software for education and business. In 1986, he also bought Lucasfilm’s computer graphics division and established it as Pixar. These were different kinds of ventures: NeXT focused on computing, while Pixar’s artists and technologists developed animation tools and films such as Toy Story.

Apple acquired NeXT in 1997, bringing Jobs back to Apple. In the following years, the company introduced products including iPod and iPhone. When introducing iPhone in 2007, Jobs described it as combining a phone, a widescreen iPod, and an internet communicator—a clear example of how he presented products to the public. Read Apple’s original announcement ↗

These products and films were team efforts. Engineers, designers, artists, and many other colleagues did the detailed work. Jobs’s career shows how a leader can set priorities and communicate a vision, while a team turns that vision into something people can use or watch.

    WHAT WE CAN LEARN

    His impact
    & lessons

    A few contributions and practical lessons from his career.

    01 / CONTRIBUTION

    Technology for everyday use

    Apple’s Macintosh, iPod, and iPhone helped more people use personal computers, carry digital music, and access the internet from a phone.

    02 / CONTRIBUTION

    Helping Pixar grow

    Jobs bought Pixar’s computer division in 1986. The studio later made Toy Story, the first feature-length film made entirely with computer animation.

    03 / LESSON

    A setback can lead to new work

    After leaving Apple, Jobs started NeXT and worked with Pixar. A change in direction can create a chance to learn and try something new.

    04 / LESSON

    Good work takes a team

    Big ideas need people with different skills. Designers, engineers, artists, and many others turn an idea into a real product.
